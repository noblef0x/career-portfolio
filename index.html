THE IDEA

Technology doesn't fail because it's complicated.

It fails when people can't use it, trust it, or depend on it. That's the space I've spent my career working in.

01

People

Listen first, stay calm, and explain technical problems in plain language so the person on the other end knows what is happening and what comes next.

02

Troubleshooting

Confirm the problem, gather the details, and work from the simplest cause to the most complex—and recognize when it is time to bring in an engineer or go onsite.

03

Documentation

Record the request, what was found, and what fixed it, and turn recurring answers into guides people can follow on their own.

04

Improvement

Look for the cause behind a repeat problem, then build a process that prevents it or makes the fix faster next time.

ONE PROJECT, ONE LESSON

Make the complicated repeatable.

Micro-LAM’s proprietary laser-guided manufacturing systems are carts that hold the laser and a Windows 10 computer running its control software. Configuring each computer by hand took about 2 hours, slowing how quickly systems reached customers.

MICRO-LAM • STANDARDIZATION

2 hours → ~30 minutes.

I created a standardized Windows 10 golden image with the required drivers, security updates, and application configuration while removing unnecessary Windows software that could interfere with the specialized environment.

The problemLong deployments created production delays, technician downtime, and inefficient use of IT resources.
The decisionStandardize the environment instead of rebuilding each system from scratch.
The outcomeApproximately 30 minutes for a fully configured machine, down from about 2 hours.

WHERE THIS IS HEADED

Technical support where the stakes are highest.

Precision manufacturing, nonprofit multi-site support, and corporate environments all taught me the same thing: people need equipment and systems they can depend on without thinking about them. In medical technology, the person waiting on a fix is caring for a patient.

WHY MEDICAL TECHNOLOGY

The same problem, a higher-stakes version.

At Micro-LAM, "the system has to work" meant protecting precision manufacturing output. At eLucid, it meant a nonprofit's staff could reliably serve their community. In medical technology support, the same discipline—careful troubleshooting, accurate documentation, and clear communication with the people depending on a fix—helps those who deliver patient care get their equipment working again. That is the work I want to do.

HOW I THINK

Clarify → Investigate → Decide → Deliver.

Good technical work isn't just knowing a tool. It's knowing how to move from ambiguity to a useful outcome.

01

Clarify

Understand the actual business or user problem before jumping to a technical solution.

02

Investigate

Gather evidence, identify constraints, isolate the cause, and understand the environment.

03

Decide

Compare practical options using reliability, security, cost, usability, and maintainability.

04

Deliver

Implement the solution, communicate clearly, document what matters, and verify the result.
